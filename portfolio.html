⌘Comparative study

How should agents coordinate under constraints?

A comparative study of Contract Net Protocol (CNP) and Distributed Constraint Satisfaction Problems (DCSP), implemented as a Python simulation using the SPADE multi-agent platform.

Multi-agent systemsSPADESimulationEvaluation
ARTIFACT 01 · RESEARCH

Research design. Agent populations were explored from 10 to 200, with constraint density from 0.1 to 0.8, across different coordination conditions.

Reported takeaway. Prior project notes describe CNP as reducing communication costs by about 34% in some dynamic settings, while DCSP performed better in tightly constrained conditions. Final public use should link the paper and validate the exact metric definitions.

Next level. Document experimental setup, provide reproducible scripts, and make benchmark data and limitations easy to inspect.

⬡Proposed architecture

DEBANAS: designing for trustworthy language models

A verification-oriented architecture exploring how large language model systems could make checks, boundaries, and the grounds for their outputs more explicit.

Trustworthy AILLMsVerificationArchitecture
ARTIFACT 02 · CONCEPT

Core question. How could a language-model system expose which checks were performed, which parts remain uncertain, and when it should defer rather than overstate confidence?

Current status. A proposed seven-component architecture, not a claim of completed empirical validation.

Next level. Define testable properties, build a prototype, and evaluate it against clear baselines and failure cases.

✳Ongoing ML research

Can models help flag recurrence risk earlier?

Exploring machine-learning approaches for early cancer recurrence prediction, with attention to validation, error analysis, and model interpretability.

Random forestXGBoostLogistic regressionExplainability
ARTIFACT 03 · ML

Evaluation plan. Compare accuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC-ROC, cross-validation, confusion matrices, and feature importance.

Explainability. Explore SHAP and LIME, with attention to whether explanations are stable and clinically interpretable.

Next level. External or temporal validation is important before drawing conclusions about generalization. This portfolio does not claim clinical readiness.

⟲Research direction

How robust is learning from demonstration?

Investigating adversarial robustness and behavioral trust when systems learn policies from examples provided by another agent or demonstrator.

Imitation learningRobustnessEvaluation
ARTIFACT 04 · ROBUSTNESS

Research question. How do small or strategic changes in demonstrations affect the policy learned by an imitation-learning system?

Potential directions. Define perturbation models, measure performance under distribution shift, and distinguish harmless variation from manipulations that change behavior.

Next level. Establish reproducible environments, controls, and clearly defined threat assumptions.

Field guide

How this portfolio works

This portfolio works like a small research game. The map leads through research missions, the coordination simulator demonstrates a toy model, and collected artifacts increase local XP.

  • Artifact collection: marks a project as explored and earns XP once per browser.
  • Scenario runs: change the toy model's outputs based on agent population, constraint density, and environment.
  • Build notes: expand with project context and any missing public links.
  • Progress: XP and badges are stored only in the current browser's local storage. No analytics or tracking are included.

Research integrity: project status is labeled explicitly. Proposed ideas are not presented as validated results, and research metrics should match the relevant manuscripts. Paper, code, and demo links are included where available.
